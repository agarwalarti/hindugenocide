Types of Crimes included in a Genocide

Genocide is made up of many kinds of crime. This article explains 21 of them, why each one counts as genocide, and gives examples from documented incidents.

The Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide defines genocide as any of five acts, committed with intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnic, racial or religious group: (a) killing members of the group; (b) causing serious bodily or mental harm to members of the group; (c) deliberately inflicting on the group conditions of life calculated to bring about its physical destruction; (d) imposing measures intended to prevent births; and (e) forcibly transferring children to another group. The full definition, and how courts have applied it, is explained in What is a Genocide?

This article goes through 21 types of crime that fall within that definition. Each one has a line on why it belongs, and examples of incidents. Every crime here counts as genocide when it is committed with the intent to destroy a group because of who its members are. Most incidents involve several of these crimes together. The 2017 massacre of Myanmar Hindus included killing, rape, arson, seizure of property, forced conversion and the flight of thousands.

Killing

Murder

Murder is the killing of Hindus, whether individually or in large numbers. The Noakhali riots of 1946 began on the day of Kojagari Lakshmi Puja and went on for about a week. Over 5,000 Hindus were killed.

Killing members of the group is the first act listed in the Genocide Convention (clause a).

Mass shooting

A mass shooting is the killing of a gathered group by gunfire. In 1979, 1,700 Hindus were gunned down at Marichjhapi in West Bengal by the state police.

Killing on this scale falls under clause (a) when the victims are targeted as a group.

Beheading

Beheading is killing by decapitation. In 1998, Islamist militants killed and beheaded 29 Hindus in the villages of Prankote and Dakikote in the Udhampur district of Jammu and Kashmir.

It is the killing of group members (clause a), and it set off the flight of more than 1,000 villagers.

Bomb Blast

A bomb blast kills and maims people gathered in one place. In 2005, serial blasts hit crowded markets in Delhi during Diwali, killing at least 64 and injuring 100. In 2006, twin blasts at the Sankat Mochan Hanuman Temple and the railway station in Varanasi killed 28 and injured 101, per official numbers.

Attacks on festivals and temples target Hindus as a community, and they bring killing and serious bodily harm (clauses a and b).

Mob Lynching

A mob lynching is a killing by a crowd, outside any legal process. On 16 April 2020 in Palghar, Maharashtra, a mob of more than 200 people attacked Hindu mahants of the Shri Panch Dashnam Juna Akhada, pelting stones and overturning their vehicle. Three were lynched.

When a crowd kills people because they belong to a group, it falls under clause (a).

Poisoning

Poisoning is harming or killing people with poison, in food, water or other substances.

It is the killing or serious bodily harm of group members (clauses a and b).

Gas chambers

Gas chambers were built to kill people in large numbers. During the Holocaust, the Nazis exterminated more than half a million Roma and Sinti people between 1938 and 1944, and some sources put the number at 1.5 million. Their culture and practices, which this site documents as largely originating in Hinduism, were also erased by law.

A people killed in numbers by design is the plainest case of clause (a).

Sexual violence

Rape

Rape is forced sexual intercourse. During the 1971 war in Bangladesh, at least 2.5 million of the 3 million Hindus in East Pakistan were slaughtered, raped or forcibly evicted from their homes.

The Rwanda tribunal ruled that rape and sexual violence constitute genocide when committed with intent to destroy a group (clause b).

Sexual Assault

Sexual assault covers sexual violence such as molestation and sexual servitude. After the 2021 elections in West Bengal, there were at least 15,000 documented cases of post-poll violence against Hindus, including 7,000 women raped or sexually molested.

The Rwanda tribunal's ruling covers sexual violence as a whole, and it causes the serious bodily and mental harm named in clause (b).

Child Molestation

Child molestation is the sexual abuse of children. In Pakistan in 2020, Muslim clerics admitted to converting over 855 Hindus in a few years, and reports record sexual assault on women and children.

Abuse of children inflicts serious bodily and mental harm on the group (clause b), and the harm reaches its youngest members.

Displacement and loss of homes and livelihood

Forced Refugees

Forced refugees are people driven from their homes by violence or threat. After US troops left Afghanistan in 2021, the remaining Hindus and Sikhs were left at the mercy of the Taliban. Of 220,000 Hindus and Sikhs, only 300 remained as of July 2021.

The Rwanda tribunal held that systematic expulsion from homes can be an act of genocide (clause c).

Seizure of assets

Seizure of assets is the taking of homes, land, businesses and wealth. In 1972, Idi Amin forced people of Indian descent, most of them Hindus, out of Uganda, and their homes were seized. About 65,000 people became refugees.

Taking away a community's means of living is a condition of life that clause (c) addresses.

Arson

Arson is the burning of homes, shops, temples and villages. In 1997, 1,391 Bru houses in 41 villages in Mizoram were burnt down, and more than 30,000 Bru people were forced out of the state.

Burning a community's homes destroys the places it lives in, and the Rwanda tribunal named expulsion from homes as a means of genocide (clause c).

Starvation

Starvation is death from lack of food. The famines of 1896–97 and 1899–1902 under British rule are estimated to have killed 19 million people.

Clause (c) covers conditions of life that destroy a group, and the Rwanda tribunal named a subsistence diet as one of them.

Bondage and forced change of faith

Slavery

Slavery is the ownership and sale of people. During Timur's attack on Delhi in 1398–99, an estimated 1.1 million Hindus were slaughtered or enslaved in a single day. In 2016, Hindu women from Andhra Pradesh and Telangana were found sold in Saudi Arabia and Bahrain for £2,000 to £4,000 each.

Enslavement strips a people of freedom, health and family life, which clauses (b) and (c) cover.

Indentured Labour

Indentured labour is work under a contract that binds a person for years. Between 1879 and 1919, the East India Company brought Hindus to Fiji as indentured labourers, and 50,000 people were exploited for labour.

Binding a community to forced work on exploitative terms is a condition of life that clause (c) addresses.

Internment

Internment is confinement in camps without trial. The Roma and Sinti people were forced into concentration camps during the Second World War.

Confining a people in camps puts them in conditions of life that clause (c) addresses.

Conversion

Conversion, in this list, means forced or deceitful change of religion. In the 1946 Noakhali riots, around 3.5 lakh Hindu men and women were forcibly converted to Islam. Between 2018 and 2026, over 3,000 Hindus from weak economic backgrounds were deceived into converting to Christianity by a Christian group.

Forced conversion ends a community's faith, the permanent crippling of a group that Lemkin described, and it is recognised as a form of cultural genocide.

Physical harm and terror

Assault

Assault is a physical attack or beating. In January 1998, 200 Indian-Guyanese were beaten in the streets of Georgetown in full view of the police, and no arrests were made.

Beatings cause the serious bodily harm named in clause (b).

Torture

Torture is the deliberate infliction of severe pain. During the conflict of 1857–58, torture was used as an act of revenge and to teach the population a lesson, and Hindus were force-fed beef in violation of their religious beliefs.

Torture causes serious bodily and mental harm (clause b), and forcing Hindus to violate their beliefs attacks the identity of the group.

Hijacking Airplane

Hijacking is the seizure of an aircraft with its passengers as hostages. In 1985, after Talwinder Singh Parmar publicly proclaimed a call to “kill 50,000 Hindus,” Air India Flight 182 from Toronto to New Delhi was bombed in Irish airspace. All 329 on board were killed, almost all of them Hindus.

Holding passengers hostage causes serious mental harm (clause b), and the Air India bombing, which followed a public call to kill Hindus, shows the intent the Convention requires.

Why the list matters

Defenders of the Convention's intent requirement argue that a pattern of purposeful action leading to the destruction of a significant part of a group is enough to establish genocidal intent. When killing, sexual violence, expulsion, loss of property and forced conversion fall on the same community over years or centuries, they form that pattern.
